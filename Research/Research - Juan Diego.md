Miracles Project - Research: Juan Diego

Basic Info:
- Name: Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin
- Born: 1474, Cuauhtitlan, Aztec Empire (modern Mexico)
- Died: 1548, Tepeyac, Mexico City
- Beatified: May 6, 1990, Basilica of Guadalupe, Mexico City (by John Paul II)
- Canonized: July 31, 2002, Basilica of Guadalupe, Mexico City (by John Paul II)
- Feast Day: December 9
- Patronage: Indigenous peoples of the Americas
- Canonization type: Confessor (equipollent beatification due to antiquity of devotion)

NOTES ON CANONIZATION PROCESS:
- Beatification was "equipollent" - dispensed from requiring a miracle due to antiquity of devotion (cult existed since 1663)
- Still required a miracle for canonization

CANONIZATION MIRACLE:
- Recipient: Juan Jose Barragan Silva, 20-year-old drug addict
- Condition: Severe trauma from 10-meter head-first fall onto cement (apparent suicide attempt)
- Injuries: Spinal column fracture, neck fracture, cranial fracture, intracranial hemorrhage
- Date of event: May 3-9, 1990 (fall on May 3, emerged from coma on May 6)
- Location: Queretaro, Mexico
- Key details: His mother Esperanza, who witnessed the fall, invoked Juan Diego to save him. He went into a coma, emerged on May 6 (the day of Juan Diego's beatification), and was discharged fully recovered a week later.
- Vatican medical board: 5 medical consultors unanimously declared it medically inexplicable (1998)
- Vatican theological board: Unanimously approved (May 2001)
- Formal approval: December 20, 2001 by Pope John Paul II
- Cure characteristics: INSTANT and COMPLETE (emerged from coma suddenly on exact beatification day)

ASSOCIATED MIRACLE: The Tilma of Our Lady of Guadalupe
- Type: Miraculous image / apparition
- Date: December 9-12, 1531
- Location: Tepeyac Hill, Mexico City
- Description: Our Lady appeared to Juan Diego, instructing him to tell Bishop Zumarraga to build a church. The bishop demanded a sign. On December 12, Juan Diego gathered Castilian roses (out of season) in his tilma (cloak). When he opened it before the bishop, the image of Our Lady of Guadalupe was miraculously imprinted on the fabric.
- Scientific study: The tilma has been studied extensively - no brushstrokes, no underdrawing on infrared, the fibers (cactus/agave) should have decayed within 20-30 years but remain intact after 500 years. The eyes reflect light in a way consistent with living human eyes. NASA scientists and other researchers have studied it without finding natural explanations.
- Note: This is a miracle associated with Juan Diego but not a posthumous intercessory miracle. It falls under the "apparition" miracle type.
