---

This is the canonization miracle for Devasahayam Pillai — the one Vatican-recognized healing required to move him from Blessed to Saint, since his beatification in 2012 had already been granted on the strength of his martyrdom alone, with the usual miracle requirement dispensed. No miracle was needed at the beatification stage; one was required for canonization, and this is it.

According to Vatican News's own English-language reporting on the February 2020 decree, the recognized miracle was "the resuscitation of a 20-week-old fetus of an Indian pregnant lady." This is the only description of the medical facts that traces to a source actually speaking for the Vatican (Vatican News is the Holy See's press outlet, distinct from — but closely tied to — the Dicastery/Congregation for the Causes of Saints that investigated the case). Several secondary Catholic-interest sites circulate a fuller narrative: that in 2013 a pregnant woman's 24-week fetus was found, on a scan, to have no heartbeat and had been declared dead in utero; that the woman, a devotee of Devasahayam, prayed for his intercession (some accounts add that she drank water from a well on the site of his family home in Nattalam village); that within about an hour she felt a change, a follow-up scan showed the fetus alive again, and roughly three months later she delivered a healthy baby boy with no complications. None of these narrative details — the well water, the one-hour timeframe, the 24-week figure, the baby's sex, the specific village — could be traced back to a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source actually fetched and read for this research; they appear consistently across several secondary summaries and blog-level accounts, which suggests a shared original report somewhere in the Indian Catholic press, but the original could not be located and confirmed directly. The discrepancy between "20 weeks" (Vatican News) and "24 weeks" (the recurring secondary narrative) is also unresolved — it's possible one number refers to gestational age at the time of the scare and the other to age at a different point, or simply that one of the two figures is a transcription drift through repeated retelling. Both are reported here rather than silently picking one.

What is solidly documented is the Vatican's procedural handling of the case. A medical board under the Congregation (now Dicastery) for the Causes of Saints examined the clinical record and, on February 28, 2019, unanimously found that the recovery could not be explained by current medical knowledge — the standard finding required before a case can proceed as a miracle for canonization purposes. The cause's theological consultors then reviewed the case at a special meeting on December 5, 2019, and the ordinary session of cardinals and bishops of the Congregation approved it on February 18, 2020. Three days later, on February 21, 2020, Pope Francis received Cardinal Angelo Becciu, then Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints, in audience and authorized the promulgation of the decree recognizing the miracle — the last formal step clearing Devasahayam for canonization. The Vatican's own press bulletin for that audience (hosted on press.vatican.va) records the authorization in exactly these terms: "il miracolo, attribuito all'intercessione del Beato Lazzaro, detto Devasahayam, Laico, Martire; nato il 23 aprile 1712 nel villaggio di Nattalam (India) e ucciso, in odio alla Fede, ad Aralvaimozhy (India) il 14 gennaio 1752" — confirming the saint's identity and dates, but, consistent with the Vatican's universal practice, naming no recipient. The underlying clinical file, like every canonization-miracle record, remains sealed within the cause's Positio in Rome and was never intended to surface recipient details publicly; the "confidential" privacy status below reflects that this isn't a gap in reporting so much as the normal condition of these cases.

Pope Francis went on to canonize Devasahayam Pillai, along with nine others, at a Mass in St. Peter's Square on May 15, 2022 — making this healing the formal basis for the first Indian layman ever to be declared a saint.
