Biography: Devasahayam Pillai

Born Neelakanda Pillai (also transliterated Nilakandan Pillai) on April 23, 1712, into an affluent Nair-caste Hindu family in the village of Nattalam, in the Kingdom of Travancore (present-day Kanyakumari district, Tamil Nadu, India), he rose to a position of trust at the royal court of Maharaja Marthanda Varma, working under the dewan Ramayyan Dalawa. His life changed after the 1741 Battle of Colachel, in which Travancore defeated a Dutch East India Company force; the captured Dutch naval commander, Eustachius De Lannoy, was pardoned and inducted into the Travancore army, where he and Neelakanda became close. During a period of personal hardship, De Lannoy introduced him to the Book of Job and the Christian understanding of suffering, and after months of instruction from the Jesuit missionary Fr. Giovanni Battista Buttari, Neelakanda was baptized in 1745, taking the name Lazarus — rendered into Tamil devotion as Devasahayam, "God is my help." His wife, Bhargavi Ammal, converted alongside him and took the name Theresa (Gnanapoo Ammal).

His conversion, and his public preaching against caste distinctions and in friendship with the lower castes and the "untouchable," scandalized the royal court and the Nair establishment; he was falsely accused of treason and espionage on behalf of the Dutch. Beginning around 1749 he was arrested, paraded in humiliation through villages, imprisoned, and subjected to prolonged, uninterrupted torture intended to force him to recant; he refused. After roughly three years of persecution and a period of forest banishment, he was taken to the fringe of the Aralvaimozhy forest and shot by firing squad in the predawn hours of January 14, 1752, at a spot known as Kattadimalai (now venerated as Devasahayam Pillai Mount). Christians recovered his body and buried it before the altar of St. Francis Xavier's Church in Kottar (now Kottar Cathedral, Nagercoil), which became the seat of a growing popular cult among Tamil and Malayali Catholics over the following two and a half centuries.

Pope Benedict XVI formally recognized his martyrdom and beatified him on December 2, 2012, in Nagercoil, in the Diocese of Kottar — as a martyr, the usual requirement of a confirmed miracle was dispensed with at this stage. Pope Francis canonized him on May 15, 2022, in St. Peter's Square, Rome, following Vatican recognition of a healing miracle attributed to his intercession, making him the first Indian layman ever raised to sainthood.
