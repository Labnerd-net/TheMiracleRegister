Biography: John Neumann

John Nepomucene Neumann was born March 28, 1811, in Prachatice, Bohemia (Austrian Empire; now Czech Republic), the third of six children of a stocking knitter. A gifted linguist, he studied at the seminary in Budweis and later at Charles-Ferdinand University in Prague, where he taught himself English in preparation for missionary work in America — he was eventually fluent in eight languages.

With his home diocese oversupplied with priests, Neumann sailed for New York with almost no money, arriving May 28, 1836, "with one tattered suit of clothes and one dollar." Bishop John Dubois ordained him a priest within a month. He spent his first years serving scattered German immigrant communities near Buffalo, traveling on foot and horseback between mission churches and enduring conditions that eventually broke his health.

In 1840 he entered the Redemptorists, becoming the congregation's first member to profess vows in the United States. He served as vice-provincial of the American Redemptorists, became a naturalized U.S. citizen in 1848, and pastored St. Alphonsus Parish in Baltimore before Pope Pius IX named him Bishop of Philadelphia in 1852.

As bishop, Neumann built the first diocesan Catholic school system in the country, growing it from one parish school to roughly 200 in less than a decade. He established Philadelphia's first Italian national parish, introduced the Forty Hours Eucharistic devotion to the diocese, and continued to live with the same personal austerity he had practiced as a missionary priest.

He died suddenly of a heart attack on January 5, 1860, while running errands in the city, at age 48. He is buried beneath the altar of St. Peter the Apostle Church in Philadelphia. Beatified October 13, 1963, and canonized June 19, 1977 — both by Pope Paul VI — he was the first American male citizen to be declared a saint.
