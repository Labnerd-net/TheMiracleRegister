Synopsis: Healing of Michael Flanigan from Bone Cancer

Saint:        John Neumann
Miracle:      Healing of Michael Flanigan from Bone Cancer
Recipient:    Michael Flanigan
Condition:    Ewing's sarcoma (metastatic bone cancer)
Date:         Diagnosed 1963, age 6; cure confirmed by Christmas 1963
Location:     West Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

---

In 1963, doctors in Philadelphia delivered the kind of diagnosis no parent is prepared to hear: six-year-old Michael Flanigan, a boy from a family belonging to Most Blessed Sacrament Parish in West Philadelphia, had Ewing's sarcoma, a bone cancer that had already spread beyond its point of origin in his leg to his jaw and his lungs. Ewing's sarcoma was, at the time, fatal in an estimated 95 percent of cases once it metastasized this widely, and the physicians treating Michael gave his parents no realistic hope of survival.

Rather than accept the prognosis, Michael's parents turned to the tomb of Bishop John Neumann, beatified only weeks earlier that October, at the Bishop Neumann Shrine inside St. Peter the Apostle Church at Fifth Street and Girard Avenue — the same church beneath whose altar Neumann's body had rested since 1860. The family made repeated visits and prayed for his intercession, and a relic connected to Neumann was kept with the boy as part of that devotion. By Christmas 1963 — the same season in which his parents had been told to expect his death — every clinical sign of the cancer that had spread through his body had disappeared.

Unlike the Benassi and Lenahan cases, which the Vatican reviewed and approved within roughly a decade of the events themselves, Michael's cure underwent a notably longer period of scrutiny — a reflection of how seriously the Congregation for the Causes of Saints treated a cancer diagnosis with confirmed metastasis, where spontaneous, unexplained remission is a phenomenon medicine does document, however rarely, and where the burden of ruling out a natural explanation is correspondingly higher. Vatican-appointed physicians reviewed Michael's complete medical record over several years, tracking his continued disease-free status well beyond the point where a recurrence would ordinarily be expected. In December 1975, the Medical Board of the Vatican's Congregation for the Causes of Saints concluded its review and declared the cure "scientifically and medically unexplainable," attributing it to the intercession of the now-Blessed John Neumann. Pope Paul VI formally approved the miracle on December 20, 1976, clearing the final obstacle to canonization. John Neumann was declared a saint in Rome on June 19, 1977 — with Michael Flanigan's recovery from a cancer that should have killed him within the year standing as the third and last of the miracles the Church required across his entire cause, following the two healings already accepted for his beatification in 1963. Flanigan, by then a 20-year-old cabinetmaker, was present as his cure — "14 years ago," as contemporary press coverage of the canonization described it — was credited as the deciding miracle. (That "14 years ago" phrasing is very likely the source of an earlier, mistaken account of this case that gave Michael's age as 14 rather than 6 — a gap-of-time figure misread as an age.)
