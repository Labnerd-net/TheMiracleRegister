Synopsis: Healing of Michael Flanigan from Bone Cancer

Saint:        John Neumann
Miracle:      Healing of Michael Flanigan from Bone Cancer
Recipient:    Michael Flanigan
Condition:    Ewing's sarcoma (metastatic bone cancer)
Date:         Onset 1962 or July 1963 (sources disagree — see open questions); cure confirmed by Christmas 1963
Location:     West Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

---

In July 1963, doctors in Philadelphia delivered the kind of diagnosis no parent is prepared to hear: Michael Flanigan, a boy from a family belonging to Most Blessed Sacrament Parish in West Philadelphia, had Ewing's sarcoma, a bone cancer that had already spread beyond its point of origin in his leg to his jaw and his lungs. Ewing's sarcoma was, in 1963, almost universally fatal once it metastasized this widely, and the physicians treating Michael gave his parents no realistic hope — by most accounts, roughly six months to live. [Note: one secondary source gives a substantially different account of this case — a younger child of five or six, injured by "bumping his shin" against a doorstep in 1962, with symptoms resolving within six weeks rather than months. It is not clear whether this is a garbled retelling of the same case or a conflation with a different one; the account followed here is the version corroborated across the Archdiocese of Philadelphia's own historical research center and multiple independent secondary sources, but this should be resolved against a primary source — the Positio or a contemporary Philadelphia newspaper — before publication.]

Rather than accept the prognosis, Michael's parents turned to the tomb of Bishop John Neumann, beatified only weeks earlier that October, at the Bishop Neumann Shrine inside St. Peter the Apostle Church at Fifth Street and Girard Avenue — the same church beneath whose altar Neumann's body had rested since 1860. The family made repeated visits and prayed for his intercession, and a relic connected to Neumann was kept with the boy as part of that devotion. By Christmas 1963 — the same season in which his parents had been told to expect his death — every clinical sign of the cancer that had spread through his body had disappeared.

Unlike the Benassi and Lenahan cases, which the Vatican reviewed and approved within roughly a decade of the events themselves, Michael's cure underwent a notably longer period of scrutiny — a reflection of how seriously the Congregation for the Causes of Saints treated a cancer diagnosis with confirmed metastasis, where spontaneous, unexplained remission is a phenomenon medicine does document, however rarely, and where the burden of ruling out a natural explanation is correspondingly higher. Vatican-appointed physicians reviewed Michael's complete medical record over several years, tracking his continued disease-free status well beyond the point where a recurrence would ordinarily be expected. In December 1975, the Medical Board of the Vatican's Congregation for the Causes of Saints concluded its review and declared the cure "scientifically and medically unexplainable," attributing it to the intercession of the now-Blessed John Neumann. Pope Paul VI formally approved the miracle on December 20, 1976, clearing the final obstacle to canonization. John Neumann was declared a saint in Rome on June 19, 1977 — with Michael Flanigan's recovery from a cancer that should have killed him within the year standing as the third and last of the miracles the Church required across his entire cause, following the two healings already accepted for his beatification in 1963.
