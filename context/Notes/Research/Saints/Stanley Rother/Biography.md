Biography: Stanley Rother

Stanley Francis Rother was born March 27, 1935, on a farm outside Okarche, Oklahoma, the eldest of four children of Franz and Gertrude Rother. Raised in the rhythms of farm labor — milking cows before dawn, working fields alongside his father — he grew up in the parish of Holy Trinity Catholic Church and felt an early call to the priesthood. That call nearly ended before it began: at St. John's Seminary in San Antonio he struggled badly with Latin and failed his first year of theology, prompting the rector to suggest he reconsider his vocation. Bishop Victor Reed gave him a second chance, sending him instead to Mount St. Mary's Seminary in Maryland, where he was ordained a priest of the Diocese of Oklahoma City and Tulsa on May 25, 1963.

After five years in Oklahoma parish assignments, Rother volunteered for the diocese's mission among the Tz'utujil Maya of Santiago Atitlán, Guatemala, arriving in 1968. Over the next 13 years he mastered Spanish and then Tz'utujil itself — a language with no written tradition — helping reduce it to writing and translating the New Testament for his parishioners. He built a farmers' co-op, a school, a clinic, and the area's first Catholic radio station, and worked beside his flock as readily as he preached to them.

As Guatemala's civil war escalated in the late 1970s, catechists and Indigenous leaders around Lake Atitlán were being disappeared and killed, and Rother's own name surfaced on a death list. He briefly retreated to Oklahoma in early 1981 but chose to return to Santiago Atitlán for Holy Week, reportedly saying that a shepherd cannot abandon his flock at the first sign of danger. On July 28, 1981, three gunmen broke into the rectory and shot him twice in the head. His body was returned to Oklahoma for burial, but at his parishioners' request his heart was entombed beneath the altar of Santiago Apóstol parish church, where it remains.

Pope Francis recognized his martyrdom — death in odium fidei — on December 1, 2016, and he was beatified September 23, 2017, in Oklahoma City before some 20,000 people: the first U.S.-born priest, and the first U.S.-born martyr, ever beatified. As a recognized martyr he was dispensed from the usual beatification-miracle requirement; canonization still awaits one verified miracle attributed to his intercession.
