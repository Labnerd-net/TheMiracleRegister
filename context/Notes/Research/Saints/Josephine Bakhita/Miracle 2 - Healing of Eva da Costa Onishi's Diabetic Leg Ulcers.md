Synopsis: Healing of Eva da Costa Onishi's Diabetic Leg Ulcers

Saint:        Josephine Bakhita
Miracle:      Healing of Eva da Costa Onishi's Diabetic Leg Ulcers
Recipient:    Eva da Costa Onishi (also rendered "Eva Tobias da Costa" in some sources)
Condition:    Infected ulcerations of the lower limbs caused by diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and chronic venous insufficiency
Date:         May 27, 1992
Location:     Santos, São Paulo state, Brazil

---

The miracle accepted by the Congregation for the Causes of Saints for Josephine Bakhita's canonization concerns Eva da Costa Onishi, a Brazilian woman born January 1, 1931, in Iguape, in the state of São Paulo. By the 1990s she was living in a poor neighborhood of Santos with her youngest son. She had been diabetic since 1976, and by 1980 had developed deep, infected ulcers on her legs — a common and serious complication of long-standing, poorly controlled diabetes, compounded in her case by hypertension and chronic venous insufficiency. Her family's limited financial means meant she could not access the level of medical care her condition required, and at least one of the wounds had progressed so severely that it reportedly reached the bone; amputation was being considered as her doctors struggled to control the infection.

Ten days after Josephine Bakhita's beatification (May 17, 1992), Eva was participating in a meeting of an "Elderly Women" devotional group at the Cathedral of Santos on May 27, 1992, when she invoked the help of the newly Blessed Bakhita. Some secondary accounts add that she had received a small holy card (santino/santinho) bearing Bakhita's image through this same parish group and placed it against her wound before going to sleep that night. Returning home after the gathering, she found that the ulcers — including the one that had reached the bone — had completely and suddenly disappeared, with the skin over the affected areas fully renewed. Her attending physician, by the account carried in Italian Catholic press, could offer no scientific explanation for what he observed.

The case was referred through the Diocese of Santos and then forwarded to the Congregation for the Causes of Saints (today the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints) in Rome for the medical and theological review required before a miracle can be formally recognized. On December 21, 1998, the Congregation issued its decree on the case, declaring the cure to be scientifically inexplicable, rapid, complete, and lasting — the standard formula used to certify a healing as miraculous for canonization purposes. With this miracle in hand, Pope John Paul II canonized Josephine Bakhita on October 1, 2000, in St. Peter's Square, as part of a Jubilee Year ceremony that also canonized Augustine Zhao Rong and 119 Companions (the China martyrs), María Josefa del Corazón de Jesús Sancho de Guerra, and Katharine Drexel. Eva da Costa herself was reported to have traveled to Rome and been present in St. Peter's Square for the canonization of the woman whose intercession she credited with saving her leg.

As with the beatification miracle, no vatican.va document naming Eva da Costa, her diagnosis, or the specific circumstances of her cure has been located. The October 1, 2000 canonization homily, read in full for this research pass, mentions Bakhita only in general, biographical and spiritual terms — calling her "a shining advocate of genuine emancipation" and recounting her childhood enslavement — without any reference to a specific miracle, medical case, or named beneficiary; the same is true of its treatment of the other three causes canonized that day. This is consistent with the project's general finding that Vatican beatification/canonization homilies are thematic rather than case-specific, and that the only place the medical case is actually documented and adjudicated — the cause's Positio, built on the Consulta Medica's findings — is never published. The December 21, 1998 Congregation decree is referenced by name and date in secondary Italian Catholic press coverage, but a copy of the decree itself was not locatable on vatican.va or elsewhere online during this research pass.

Because this miracle was formally investigated and confirmed by the Congregation for the Causes of Saints and used to canonize Bakhita, its `approval_authority` is `vatican_dicastery` and `used_for_canonization` is true.
