Synopsis: Healing of Sister Mari Silla's Knee Synovitis

Saint:        Josephine Bakhita
Miracle:      Healing of Sister Mari Silla's Knee Synovitis
Recipient:    Sister Mari Silla (Canossian Daughter of Charity)
Condition:    Osteoarthritis and specific synovitis of the left knee
Date:         October 22, 1947 [date as given by Italian-language secondary sources; see conflicting account below]
Location:     Pavia, Italy [disputed — see note below]

---

The miracle accepted by the Congregation for the Causes of Saints as the basis for Josephine Bakhita's beatification concerns Sister Mari Silla, a Canossian Daughter of Charity, who according to Italian-language secondary sources was instantaneously healed on October 22, 1947 — a little over eight months after Bakhita's own death on February 8, 1947 — of what is described as "osteoartrite e sinovite specifica al ginocchio sinistro" (osteoarthritis and specific synovitis of the left knee). The healing is reported as sudden and complete, consistent with the "instant, complete" cure pattern the Vatican's medical consultors typically require before a case can even be forwarded to the theological consultors for a judgment of miraculous character.

Multiple English-language secondary accounts tell a version of this same story with details that do not fully match the Italian-language accounts, and it has not been possible to resolve the discrepancy from the sources gathered so far. In the English-language retellings (traceable to a review of Véronique Olmi's historical novel "Bakhita," and repeated across several devotional and catalog sites), the sister is named "Angela Silla," is described as a "Paduan" Canossian sister (i.e. from Padua, not Pavia), and her condition is given as "tuberculosis of the knee" rather than synovitis/osteoarthritis — with the detail that she was healed "the day before she was due to have her leg amputated." A related but distinct cluster of English retellings (traceable to popular devotional/apologetics sites rather than the novel review) describes a nun who suffered nine years of bedridden "arthritic synovitis," who awoke the night before a scheduled operation to a voice saying "Get up and walk," and whose post-cure X-ray showed no trace of disease — placing the healing in 1948 rather than 1947, and describing an avoided operation rather than an avoided amputation.

All three versions agree on the essential shape of the case — a Canossian sister suffering from a knee ailment, healed suddenly and inexplicably shortly before a scheduled surgical intervention, with the cure attributed to Bakhita's intercession and accepted as the beatification miracle — but they disagree on her first name (Mari vs. Angela), her community/city (Pavia vs. Padua), the precise diagnosis (synovitis/osteoarthritis vs. tuberculosis of the knee vs. unspecified "arthritic synovitis"), the year (1947 vs. 1948), and the surgery she was due to undergo (unspecified operation vs. leg amputation vs. knee operation). The Italian-language sources (Famiglia Cristiana and other sources citing the same underlying account) are more specific and internally consistent with each other on the name, location, and diagnosis, and this synopsis defaults to that version as the primary account, but neither version has been corroborated against a Vatican-published primary document — see Open Questions.

As with essentially every Vatican-recognized healing miracle, the underlying medical dossier is not public. The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints' Consulta Medica evaluates these cases from within the cause's sealed Positio, and no vatican.va page for this cause names Sister Silla, her diagnosis, or the date of her cure. Pope John Paul II's May 17, 1992 beatification homily (delivered jointly for Bakhita and Josemaría Escrivá de Balaguer, who were beatified the same day) speaks only in general terms about Bakhita's life and spiritual significance; it does not mention a specific miracle or recipient. A separate papal address to pilgrims the following day (18 May 1992, in Italian, addressed specifically to those who had come to Rome for Bakhita's beatification) likewise contains no reference to the healed sister, her condition, or the circumstances of the cure. This is consistent with the project's general finding that older causes, especially from the mid-20th century, typically have only a thematic beatification homily rather than a case-specific decree page — the Vatican document that would name the recipient is the sealed Positio itself, not anything published on vatican.va.

Because the healing was accepted and formally recognized as the beatification miracle, its `approval_authority` is `vatican_dicastery` and both `used_for_beatification` is true. No vatican.va source naming this specific case has been located to satisfy the Tier 1 bar described in the project's sourcing standard; the sources gathered here are Tier 2 Italian Catholic press and aggregator sites repeating the same underlying facts, which is the ceiling available for a cause of this age absent access to the sealed Positio.
