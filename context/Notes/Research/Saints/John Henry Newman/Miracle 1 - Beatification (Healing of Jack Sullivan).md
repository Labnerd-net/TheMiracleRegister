Jack Sullivan, a permanent-deacon candidate from Marshfield, Massachusetts, developed severe pain and weakness in his legs in the summer of 2000. In late July, the chief neurosurgeon at Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston told him that his lower spinal cord — normally about the size of a quarter — had been compressed by his vertebrae and discs down to the width of a piece of string, and was so badly damaged it would never decompress. Surgery was scheduled, but in the meantime Sullivan was left in severe pain and largely unable to walk.

While watching a television program about English cardinals, Sullivan learned of John Henry Newman and began praying a novena for his intercession, asking that he be able to walk again in time for his surgery and, eventually, to complete diaconate formation and be ordained. On August 15, 2001 — the day before a subsequent surgery — Sullivan reported a sudden and complete relief of pain, finding he could stand and walk normally for the first time in over a year. His doctors, who had expected a difficult recovery even after successful surgery, could not account for the abrupt and complete resolution of a condition they had regarded as effectively untreatable outside of the operation itself.

Sullivan went on to complete his diaconate formation and was ordained a permanent deacon for the Archdiocese of Boston. The Archdiocese opened a formal investigation into the healing, forwarding the case file to the Congregation for the Causes of Saints in Rome in August 2006. On April 24, 2008, a panel of Vatican medical consultants voted unanimously that the recovery "defied any scientific or medical explanation." A year later, on April 24, 2009, the Congregation's theological consultors unanimously attributed the healing to Newman's intercession. Pope Benedict XVI formally recognized the cure as a miracle on July 3, 2009, clearing the way for Newman's beatification, which the pope celebrated in person at Cofton Park, Birmingham, on September 19, 2010.
