John Henry Newman was born on February 21, 1801, at 80 Old Broad Street in the City of London, the eldest of six children. Raised in the Church of England, he underwent an evangelical conversion at age fifteen and went on to Trinity College, Oxford, later becoming a fellow of Oriel College. Ordained an Anglican priest in 1825, he became vicar of St Mary's, Oxford, and emerged as the intellectual leader of the Oxford Movement, a group of churchmen seeking to recover the Catholic character of Anglicanism through the Tracts for the Times. His 1841 Tract 90, arguing that the Thirty-Nine Articles could be read in harmony with Catholic doctrine, provoked a storm of controversy and effectively ended his Oxford career.

After years of study and doubt, Newman was received into the Catholic Church on October 9, 1845. He traveled to Rome, was ordained a Catholic priest in 1847, and joined the Oratory of St Philip Neri, founding its first English house at Maryvale near Birmingham in 1848 before settling the community at Edgbaston, where the Birmingham Oratory remains today.

Newman went on to serve as the founding rector of the Catholic University of Ireland in Dublin, where his lectures became the basis for his classic work on liberal education, The Idea of a University. In 1864, responding to a public attack on his honesty by Charles Kingsley, he wrote Apologia Pro Vita Sua, a spiritual autobiography that won him wide respect even among Protestants and remains one of the most admired works of English religious literature. He also wrote An Essay in Aid of a Grammar of Assent, a study of the psychology of religious belief, and the poem The Dream of Gerontius, later set to music by Edward Elgar.

In 1879, Pope Leo XIII made Newman a cardinal in recognition of his services to the Church, allowing him to keep his residence at the Birmingham Oratory rather than move to Rome. He chose as his cardinatial motto "Cor ad cor loquitur" — "Heart speaks to heart." He died at the Oratory in Edgbaston on August 11, 1890.

His cause for canonization opened in 1958. Pope John Paul II declared him Venerable in 1991. He was beatified by Pope Benedict XVI in Birmingham on September 19, 2010, and canonized by Pope Francis in St Peter's Square on October 13, 2019. On November 1, 2025, Pope Leo XIV declared Newman a Doctor of the Church — the first Englishman so honored since the Venerable Bede — and named him, alongside St. Thomas Aquinas, co-patron of the Church's educational mission.
