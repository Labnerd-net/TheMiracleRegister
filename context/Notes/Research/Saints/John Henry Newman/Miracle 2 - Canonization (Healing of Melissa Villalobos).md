Melissa Villalobos, a lawyer and mother in the Chicago area, began bleeding early in her fifth pregnancy in the spring of 2013. Doctors found that her placenta had partially detached from the uterine wall, leaving a hole that allowed blood to escape. To make matters worse, she also developed a subchorionic hematoma — a separate blood clot lodged in the fetal membrane that grew to roughly two and a half times the size of the baby. On May 10, 2013, worsening bleeding sent her to the emergency room; doctors could not stop it and warned her that both her life and her unborn child's were at serious risk.

On the morning of May 15, 2013, Villalobos woke to find herself hemorrhaging heavily and, alone in the house with her older children and too weak to call for help, she prayed aloud: "Please, Cardinal Newman, stop the bleeding." She later said that as soon as she finished speaking, the bleeding stopped completely, and she noticed an overpowering scent of roses in the room — a scent she associated with Newman devotion — which recurred a second time that day. At a doctor's appointment later that same afternoon, an ultrasound confirmed what physicians could not explain: the bleeding had resolved and the pregnancy was no longer in danger.

Villalobos carried the pregnancy to term, delivering a healthy daughter, Gemma, on December 27, 2013. The Archdiocese of Chicago opened an investigation into the healing, and the case was forwarded to Rome. Vatican medical consultants found no natural explanation for the sudden and complete resolution, and in November 2018 the case was approved as a miracle. Pope Francis signed the decree on February 13, 2019, clearing the way for Newman's canonization, which he celebrated in St Peter's Square on October 13, 2019 — an event Villalobos and her family, including Gemma, attended in person.
