Synopsis: Healing of Fr. Michael Driscoll from Metastatic Melanoma

Saint:        Titus Brandsma
Miracle:      Healing of Fr. Michael Driscoll from Metastatic Melanoma
Recipient:    Fr. Michael Driscoll, O.Carm.
Condition:    Stage IV metastatic melanoma (spread to the lymph nodes)
Date:         2004 (diagnosis and surgery); healing/remission confirmed as lasting over the following 15+ years
Location:     Boca Raton, Florida, USA (residence/parish; see note on surgery location below)

---

This is the canonization miracle for Titus Brandsma — the single Vatican-recognized healing that moved him from Blessed (1985) to Saint (2022). As a martyr, he had been dispensed from the usual miracle requirement at beatification; canonization still required one, and this case supplied it.

In 2004, Fr. Michael Driscoll, a Carmelite priest then serving as pastor of St. Jude Parish in Boca Raton, Florida (he had previously served at St. Joan of Arc Parish in Boca Raton since 1977), was 62 years old when a dermatologist, examining a bump on his head, discovered what proved to be Stage IV metastatic melanoma — an unusually aggressive form of skin cancer that had already spread to his lymph nodes. Melanoma at this stage carries a grim prognosis; metastatic spread to multiple lymph nodes is generally considered very difficult to treat successfully. About a month before his scheduled surgery, Driscoll was given a small relic: a fragment of black cloth from Titus Brandsma's Carmelite habit. Because Brandsma had been cremated at Dachau, no bodily relics of him exist, so cloth relics of this kind are the primary physical means by which his intercession is invoked. Driscoll applied the relic and prayed for Brandsma's intercession in the lead-up to his operation.

In late July 2004, Driscoll underwent an eleven-hour surgery at Massachusetts General Hospital in Boston, during which surgeons removed 84 lymph nodes along with a salivary gland in an effort to excise the cancer's spread. He then underwent 35 sessions of radiation treatment. Given the stage and extent of the disease, doctors reportedly considered his prognosis poor and his eventual full recovery medically surprising; at a later checkup his physician is reported to have told him simply, "You're cured." Driscoll's cancer did not recur. By the time Church investigators examined the case, his melanoma-free survival had extended past fifteen years with no trace of recurrence — a duration and completeness of remission from Stage IV metastatic melanoma that the Vatican's own medical consultants would later describe as inexplicable by current medical knowledge.

The Diocese of Palm Beach opened a formal canonical inquiry into the case, conducted under Bishop Gerald M. Barbarito with Msgr. (Archimandrite) Glen J. Pothier serving as episcopal delegate/judicial vicar and Fr. Albert Dello Russo as promoter of justice. The inquiry ran from July 11, 2016, and held its formal closing session in December 2017, compiling nearly 1,300 pages of testimony from physicians, colleagues, and parishioners. In February 2018, representatives of the Carmelite Order carried the sealed documentation — reported as 1,232 pages — to Rome for delivery to the Dicastery (then Congregation) for the Causes of Saints.

In Rome, the Dicastery's Consulta Medica (medical board) reviewed the clinical record and, at its session on November 26, 2020, concluded — in the Dicastery's own words, as published on its official case page — that "quella guarigione fu rapida, completa e duratura, e non trova spiegazione nelle attuali conoscenze della medicina" ("that healing was rapid, complete and lasting, and finds no explanation in current medical knowledge"). The cause's theological consultors affirmed the healing as a miracle attributable to Brandsma's intercession on May 25, 2021, and the cardinals and bishops of the Dicastery, meeting in ordinary session, concurred on November 9, 2021. On November 25, 2021 — Thanksgiving Day in the United States — Pope Francis authorized Cardinal Marcello Semeraro, the Dicastery's prefect, to promulgate the decree recognizing the miracle, clearing the final procedural step toward canonization. Pope Francis canonized Titus Brandsma, together with nine others, at a Mass in St. Peter's Square on May 15, 2022.

No Vatican-published document — not the November 2021 press bulletin announcing the decree, nor the Dicastery's own case page, nor Pope Francis's canonization homily — names Fr. Driscoll, his diagnosis, or the specific circumstances of his cure. This is consistent with standard Vatican practice across canonization miracles generally: the underlying clinical record and Consulta Medica findings remain sealed within the cause's Positio in Rome, and the public decree confirms only that a miracle was recognized, identifying the saint but never the recipient. The recipient's identity, diagnosis, and narrative detail recounted above come entirely from Catholic press reporting (Catholic World Report, EWTN, National Catholic Register, Florida Catholic) published around the November 2021 decree and the May 2022 canonization, plus the Diocese of Palm Beach's own account of its tribunal process — not from a Vatican-published primary document naming the case.

One geographic detail is worth flagging explicitly: Driscoll's parish and residence, and the diocesan tribunal that investigated the case, were in the Diocese of Palm Beach, Florida — but the surgery itself took place at Massachusetts General Hospital in Boston. Catholic press accounts consistently frame this as "a Florida miracle" (the CNA/EWTN headline is explicit: "a Florida priest urges Catholic leaders..."), reflecting where Driscoll lived, prayed, and was later examined by diocesan investigators, even though the acute medical intervention happened in Massachusetts. This research follows that framing for Location Name below but flags the Boston surgery site as a fact worth keeping distinct.
