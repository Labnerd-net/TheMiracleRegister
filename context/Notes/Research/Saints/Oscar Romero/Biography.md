Biography: Oscar Romero

Óscar Arnulfo Romero y Galdámez was born August 15, 1917, in Ciudad Barrios, El Salvador, the second of eight children in a family of modest means; his father worked as a telegraph operator, and Oscar trained as a carpenter before entering the minor seminary in San Miguel at age twelve or thirteen. He continued formation at the national seminary in San Salvador, then studied theology at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome from 1937, remaining through the Second World War. He was ordained a priest on April 4, 1942, and returned to El Salvador, where he spent more than twenty years in parish ministry in the Diocese of San Miguel before becoming Secretary of the Episcopal Conference of El Salvador in 1967. He was named auxiliary bishop of San Salvador in 1970, Bishop of Santiago de María in 1974, and Archbishop of San Salvador on February 3, 1977 — initially regarded as a cautious, conservative choice amid the country's escalating political violence.

That reputation changed within weeks. The March 12, 1977 assassination of his close friend, Jesuit priest Rutilio Grande, who had been organizing poor farmworkers, marked a turning point: Romero became an outspoken defender of the poor, documenting human rights abuses, disappearances, and killings by the military and allied death squads in weekly radio homilies broadcast nationwide. On March 23, 1980, he directly appealed to Salvadoran soldiers to disobey orders to kill civilians. The next day, March 24, 1980, he was shot and killed by a sniper while celebrating Mass at the chapel of the Hospital of the Divine Providence in San Salvador — a killing a United Nations Truth Commission later concluded had been ordered by a death-squad leader tied to the military establishment.

Pope Francis recognized Romero's death as martyrdom "in odium fidei" on February 3, 2015, a declaration that, per canon law for martyrs, waived the usual requirement of a miracle for beatification. He was beatified in San Salvador on May 23, 2015. For canonization, martyrs require verification of only one miracle rather than the usual two; that requirement was fulfilled by the Vatican's March 6, 2018 decree recognizing the medically inexplicable recovery of Cecilia Flores de Rivas. Pope Francis canonized him on October 14, 2018, alongside Pope Paul VI, making him the first Salvadoran saint.
