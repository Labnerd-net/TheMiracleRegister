Biography: Marguerite d'Youville

Marguerite Dufrost de Lajemmerais was born October 15, 1701, in Varennes, in what was then New France, the eldest of six children. Her father died when she was seven, leaving the family in financial hardship; she was educated for two years by the Ursulines in Quebec City before returning home to help raise her younger siblings. In 1722, at 21, she married Francois d'Youville, a merchant later revealed to be involved in illegal liquor trading with Indigenous communities. The marriage was difficult and largely unhappy. She bore six children, four of whom died in infancy, before her husband died in 1730, leaving her a widow at 29 with significant debts.

Rather than retreat from public life, d'Youville turned to caring for the poor of Montreal, initially alongside a small group of companions who pooled resources to shelter and feed the destitute despite open public hostility and ridicule (local gossip for a time nicknamed them "les grises," a term later reclaimed as the Grey Nuns' own name). In 1737, she and three companions consecrated themselves to this work, the founding act of what became the Sisters of Charity of Montreal. In 1747, she and her sisters took over administration of the failing General Hospital of Montreal (Hopital General), rebuilding its finances and operations and expanding its care to the poor, the elderly, abandoned infants, and people with disabilities, without regard to religion or background. The hospital burned down in 1765; d'Youville rebuilt it.

She died December 23, 1771, in Montreal. Pope John XXIII beatified her on May 3, 1959, giving her the title "Mother of Universal Charity." Pope John Paul II canonized her on December 9, 1990, making her the first person born in Canada to be declared a saint.
