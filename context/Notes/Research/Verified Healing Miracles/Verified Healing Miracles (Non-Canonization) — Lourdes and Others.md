# Verified Healing Miracles (Non-Canonization) — Lourdes and Others

Sources: Sanctuary of Lourdes Medical Bureau, CMIL (Comité Médical International de Lourdes), Miracle Hunter
Date compiled: June 6, 2026

---

## Overview

These are healing miracles that were formally investigated and declared miraculous by the Church, but NOT as part of a beatification or canonization process for an individual. The most famous are the Lourdes healings, verified by the unique Medical Bureau of Lourdes.

---

## Lourdes — 70 Officially Recognized Miraculous Healings

**Context:** Founded in 1883, the Bureau des Constatations Médicales de Lourdes is the only permanent medical investigation office at any religious shrine in the world. More than 7,000 healings have been reported since 1858; 70 have been declared miraculous.

**Criteria for Recognition (7 criteria):**
1. The disease must be incurable or serious
2. It must be organic (not functional)
3. No treatment was given that could explain the cure
4. The cure must be sudden and instantaneous
5. It must be complete (not partial)
6. No convalescence — immediate recovery
7. The cure must be lasting and definitive

**Demographics:**
- 80% women
- Youngest: 2 years old
- Countries: France (56), Italy (8), Belgium (3), Germany (1), Austria (1), Switzerland (1)
- ~50 were healed by contact with Lourdes water at the baths
- 6 people claim healing by intercession without having visited Lourdes

**Notable Cases:**
- **Catherine Latapie** (1858) — First miracle; crushed nerves in her hand restored after immersing in the spring Bernadette discovered
- **Louis Bouriette** (1858) — Blindness healed after washing with Lourdes water
- **Pierre de Rudder** (1875) — Broken leg (compound fracture for 8 years) instantly healed; his healed leg showed the old fracture lines still visible in X-rays years later
- **Gabriel Gargam** (1901) — Tuberculosis peritonitis cured immediately after drinking Lourdes water
- **Marie Lemarchand** (1942) — Lupus vulgaris (tuberculosis of the face) healed instantaneously
- **Vittorio Micheli** (1963, Italy) — Bone cancer of the hip regenerated entirely; X-ray documentation exists
- **Jean-Pierre Bély** (1972) — Multiple sclerosis, cured instantly
- **Serge Perrin** (1976) — Multiple debilitating conditions including blindness; healed at the baths
- **Delizia Cirolli** (1976, Italy) — Bone cancer of the knee (Ewing's sarcoma) disappeared
- **Bernadette Moriau** (2008) — Cauda equina syndrome (paralysis of lower body, nerve damage) — officially recognized in 2018, the most recent declared miracle

## Other Verified Healing Miracles (Non-Lourdes)

While most formal miracle declarations happen in canonization processes, there are some notable healing miracles at other shrines that received local episcopal recognition:

- **Our Lady of the Rosary of Pompeii** — Several documented healings tied to the Pompeii shrine
- **St. Anne de Beaupré** (Quebec, Canada) — The shrine documents numerous healings; a few have received formal recognition
- **Our Lady of Victories** (Paris) — The "Miraculous Medal" has been associated with numerous healings since 1830
- **Our Lady of Walsingham** (England) — Some healings recognized
- **Our Lady of Licheń** (Poland) — Healing documented
- **St. Padre Pio's intercession** — Numerous healings documented by the Vatican; some have been used for beatification miracles
- **St. Jude Shrine** (Chicago / various) — Devotion to St. Jude ("saint of hopeless causes") is associated with many claimed healings, though most are at the level of reported favors rather than formally investigated miracles

---

## Key Distinction

These Lourdes healings are NOT canonization miracles. They are recognized by the bishop of the healed person's home diocese based on the CMIL's medical finding that the cure is "unexplained in the present state of our knowledge." The healings are attributed to the intercession of Our Lady of Lourdes.

Canonization miracles, by contrast, are specifically attributed to the intercession of a candidate for sainthood and are required as part of the beatification/canonization process.

## Notes
- The 70 Lourdes miracles are the largest single collection of formally recognized healing miracles outside the canonization process
- Other than Lourdes, most formally recognized healing miracles DO occur as part of canonization causes
- The distinction matters: canonization miracles require attribution to a specific saint; Lourdes miracles are attributed to Our Lady of Lourdes
