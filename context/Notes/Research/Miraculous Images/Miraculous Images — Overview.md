## Most Famous Miraculous Images

### Our Lady of Guadalupe (1531, Mexico City)
- **Type:** Tilma (cloak) made of cactus fiber
- The image on Juan Diego's tilma has defied scientific explanation for 500 years
- No paint source found; fibers not painted but somehow colored
- Eyes reflect the room as it was in 1531 (multiple people identified in the corneas)
- Tilma material should have decayed within 30 years; still intact
- Bomb tested 10 feet away in 1921 — the tilma was unharmed

### Our Lady of Czestochowa (1384, Poland)
- **Type:** Icon (painted on wood)
- Known as the "Black Madonna" — darkened by centuries of varnish and smoke
- Survived fires, wars, and sword cuts (scars still visible)
- Connected to Poland's miraculous defense against Swedish invasion (1655)

### Our Lady of the Snows / Salus Populi Romani (352, Rome)
- **Type:** Icon — said to be painted by St. Luke
- Housed at St. Mary Major, one of the four major Roman basilicas
- Miracle of the Snow: snow fell in August 352, marking the church's foundation

### Our Lady of Montserrat (1325, Spain)
- **Type:** Dark wooden statue
- Known as "La Moreneta" (the little dark one)
- One of the most venerated images in Spain; shrine a major pilgrimage site

### Our Lady of Las Lajas (1754, Colombia)
- **Type:** Painting/Fresco on cave rock
- Image appeared burned into a rock face; no paint source identified
- The rock underneath was tested — the image goes INTO the rock, not on top of it
- Basilica built around the image; still examined by scientists

### Our Lady of the Pillar (40 A.D., Zaragoza, Spain)
- **Type:** Statue on a pillar
- Earliest Marian image — tradition says Mary appeared to St. James the Greater
- The pillar itself is still venerated in the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar

## Notes
- The Church does not worship images (as clarified at the Second Council of Nicaea, 787 A.D.)
- Veneration of images is permitted: "the honor shown to them is referred to the prototypes which they represent" (Council of Trent)
- Many miraculous images originated through unknown artists, sudden appearances, or restoration that revealed beauty
- Images can be associated with a range of phenomena: weeping, bleeding, uncanny preservation, supernatural origin, or answered prayers at the site
