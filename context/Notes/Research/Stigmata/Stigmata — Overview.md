# Stigmata — Overview

Sources: Miracle Hunter (miraclehunter.com/stigmata/), Catholic Encyclopedia (New Advent), Wikipedia
Date compiled: June 6, 2026

---

## Total: ~320 recorded stigmatics in Church history

First recorded case: St. Francis of Assisi (1224). Roughly 321 counted by Dr. Imbert in the Catholic Encyclopedia (1912), including 41 men and 62 saints/blesseds. Over 80% are women. ~66% entered religious life. None prior to the 13th century.

---

## Geographic Distribution
- Italy: more than 1/3 of all cases
- Spain, France, Portugal: most of the remainder
- 20th century saw wider distribution: Germany, Belgium, Poland, Canada, USA, Mexico, Chile, Syria, Vietnam, India

## Key Facts
- The Church does NOT take stigmata as automatic proof of holiness — cases are investigated carefully
- True stigmata are extremely painful, don't have foul odor, don't respond to medical treatment
- The most common type includes wounds on hands, feet, side, and forehead (crown of thorns)
- Some stigmatics have "invisible stigmata" — the pain without visible wounds
- Associated phenomena often include ecstasies, visions, inedia, and healing powers
