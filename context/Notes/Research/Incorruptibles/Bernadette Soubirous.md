Synopsis: Incorruptibility of Saint Bernadette Soubirous

Saint:        Bernadette Soubirous
Miracle:      The Incorrupt Body of Saint Bernadette Soubirous
Type:         incorruptibility
Category:     associated
Date:         September 22, 1909 (first exhumation and discovery)
Location:     Convent of Saint-Gildard, Nevers, France

---

Bernadette Soubirous died at the Convent of Saint-Gildard in Nevers on April 16, 1879, aged 35, after years of suffering from tuberculosis of the bone and other chronic ailments. She was buried in the convent's Chapel of St. Joseph. Thirty years later, on September 22, 1909, her body was exhumed as part of the formal canonization cause. What those present found surprised them: the corpse showed no significant decomposition. The skin was desiccated but intact, the limbs had not collapsed, and the face remained recognizable. The crucifix in her hands and her rosary had oxidized, but the body within the coffin showed none of the advanced putrefaction expected after three decades in the ground. Bishop Gauthey of Nevers presided over the examination in the presence of doctors, notaries, and members of the community. The body was washed, reclothed, and reburied in a new double casket.

A second exhumation on April 3, 1919, found the body further preserved, though showing the effects of time: patches of mold, a layer of mineral salts, and partial skin loss in certain areas. The examining physician, Dr. Comte, described it as "practically mummified," noting the consistency of the remaining tissue. Despite the changes, the Church's commission determined the body continued to meet the traditional definition of incorruptibility — the absence of putrefaction and the retention of recognizable form.

The third and final exhumation took place on April 18, 1925 — forty-six years after death — in preparation for beatification. Doctors Comte and Talon described the remaining soft tissue as having "a soft and almost normal consistency." Relics were collected at this time: fragments of ribs, both kneecaps, a muscle sample, hair, and skin. Because the face and hands had darkened and contracted, the Church commissioned Parisian sculptor Pierre Imans to create a wax facial mask and wax hands to preserve the body's dignity for public veneration.

Since 1925, the incorrupt body of Saint Bernadette has rested in a crystal reliquary in the main chapel of the Convent of Saint-Gildard in Nevers, visited by hundreds of thousands of pilgrims each year. The preservation is attested by three independent medical examinations spanning forty-six years.
