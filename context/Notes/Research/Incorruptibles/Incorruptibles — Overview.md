# Incorruptibles — Overview

Sources: Miracle Hunter (miraclehunter.com/incorruptibles/), Joan Carroll Cruz "The Incorruptibles" (TAN Books), Wikipedia Category:Incorrupt saints
Date compiled: June 6, 2026

---

## Total: ~100-150+ documented cases

Bodies of saints that have not naturally decayed, often for centuries. The Church estimates roughly 1% of all canonized saints are incorrupt. Joan Carroll Cruz's book documents over 100.

## Most Visited / Famous
- St. Bernadette (Nevers, France) — body exhumed 3 times, perfectly preserved
- St. Catherine Labouré (Paris) — incorrupt since 1876
- St. John Vianney (Ars) — body in a wax mask reliquary
- St. Cecilia (Rome) — body discovered in 1599, still intact after 1,400+ years
- St. Francis Xavier (Goa, India) — body taken to Goa, still visible
- St. Teresa of Avila (Alba de Tormes, Spain) — incorrupt heart and hand
- St. Francis de Sales (Annecy, France)
- St. Vincent de Paul (Paris) — incorrupt, exhibited every 100 years
- St. John Bosco (Turin, Italy)
- St. Charbel Makhlouf (Lebanon) — body exuded blood and oil for decades

---

## Notes
- Incorruptibility is not automatic proof of holiness (exceptions exist), but it is treated as a remarkable sign.
- Many incorrupt bodies are displayed in glass reliquaries in churches.
- The formal investigation of incorruptibility sometimes occurs during the canonization process.
