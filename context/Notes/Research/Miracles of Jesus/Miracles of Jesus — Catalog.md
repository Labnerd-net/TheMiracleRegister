Miracles of Jesus — Complete Scriptural Catalog

A comprehensive listing of the miracles attributed to Jesus Christ in the four canonical Gospels. Organized roughly in canonical Gospel order, with cross-references across Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John.

Note: Dates are not generally available for individual miracles. Locations are specified where known; "Galilee" is used as a general region where the specific town is not named.


MIRACLES OF NATURE

1. Water into Wine
   Description: At a wedding feast in Cana, Jesus transforms six stone jars of water into wine after the host runs out. This is described as the "first of his signs" — the beginning of his public ministry.
   Location: Cana, Galilee
   Reference: John 2:1-11

2. First Miraculous Catch of Fish
   Description: After a night of catching nothing, Jesus tells Simon Peter to lower his nets one more time. They catch so many fish the nets begin to tear. Peter falls to his knees in awe, and Jesus calls him to become a "fisher of men."
   Location: Sea of Galilee (near Capernaum)
   Reference: Luke 5:1-11

3. Calming the Storm
   Description: While crossing the Sea of Galilee, a furious storm arises and swamps the boat. Jesus rebukes the wind and the waves, and there is a great calm. His disciples marvel, asking "What kind of man is this? Even the winds and the waves obey him!"
   Location: Sea of Galilee
   Reference: Matthew 8:23-27; Mark 4:35-41; Luke 8:22-25

4. Feeding of the 5,000
   Description: A large crowd follows Jesus to a remote place. With only five barley loaves and two fish from a boy, Jesus gives thanks, breaks the bread, and distributes to the crowd. All eat and are satisfied, and twelve baskets of leftovers are collected. The only miracle recorded in all four Gospels.
   Location: Bethsaida, near the Sea of Galilee
   Reference: Matthew 14:13-21; Mark 6:30-44; Luke 9:10-17; John 6:1-15

5. Walking on Water
   Description: During the fourth watch of the night, Jesus walks across the Sea of Galilee to reach his disciples' boat, which is struggling against the wind. Peter briefly walks on water toward Jesus before sinking in fear. When Jesus enters the boat, the storm ceases.
   Location: Sea of Galilee
   Reference: Matthew 14:22-33; Mark 6:45-52; John 6:16-21

6. Feeding of the 4,000
   Description: A crowd of four thousand men (plus women and children) has been with Jesus for three days with nothing to eat. Jesus has compassion and multiplies seven loaves and a few small fish. Seven baskets remain after everyone is satisfied.
   Location: Decapolis region, near the Sea of Galilee
   Reference: Matthew 15:32-39; Mark 8:1-10

7. The Fig Tree Cursed
   Description: On the morning after the Triumphal Entry, Jesus sees a fig tree with leaves but no fruit and curses it, saying "May no fruit ever come from you again." The tree withers immediately, and Jesus uses it to teach about faith and prayer.
   Location: Bethany, on the road between Bethany and Jerusalem
   Reference: Matthew 21:18-22; Mark 11:12-25

8. Coin in the Fish's Mouth
   Description: When collectors of the temple tax ask Peter whether Jesus pays the two-drachma tax, Jesus tells Peter to go to the lake and cast a hook. The first fish he catches has a four-drachma coin in its mouth — enough to pay the tax for both of them.
   Location: Capernaum
   Reference: Matthew 17:24-27

9. Second Miraculous Catch of Fish (Post-Resurrection)
   Description: After the resurrection, the disciples are fishing on the Sea of Tiberias but catch nothing. The risen Jesus, unrecognized on the shore, tells them to cast the net on the right side. They catch 153 large fish, and the net does not tear. John recognizes Jesus and says "It is the Lord!"
   Location: Sea of Tiberias (Sea of Galilee)
   Reference: John 21:1-14

10. Transfiguration
    Description: Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain. His face shines like the sun and his clothes become dazzling white. Moses and Elijah appear and speak with him about his impending departure (exodus) in Jerusalem. A voice from heaven says "This is my beloved Son, listen to him."
    Location: Mount Tabor (traditional identification) or Mount Hermon (alternative)
    Reference: Matthew 17:1-8; Mark 9:2-8; Luke 9:28-36


HEALING MIRACLES

11. Healing of the Nobleman's Son
    Description: A royal official from Capernaum begs Jesus to heal his dying son. Jesus says "Go, your son will live." The man believes Jesus' word and departs; his servants meet him on the way with news that the son recovered at the very hour Jesus spoke.
    Location: Cana (where Jesus was) / Capernaum (where the boy was)
    Reference: John 4:46-54

12. Healing of a Man with an Unclean Spirit
    Description: In the synagogue at Capernaum, a man with an unclean spirit cries out, "What have you to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are — the Holy One of God!" Jesus rebukes the spirit and commands it to come out. The crowd is amazed at his authority.
    Location: Capernaum (synagogue)
    Reference: Mark 1:23-28; Luke 4:33-37

13. Healing of Peter's Mother-in-Law
    Description: Jesus enters Peter's house and finds his mother-in-law sick with a high fever. He touches her hand, rebukes the fever, and it leaves her immediately. She gets up and begins serving them.
    Location: Capernaum (Simon Peter's house)
    Reference: Matthew 8:14-17; Mark 1:29-31; Luke 4:38-39

14. Healing of a Man with Leprosy
    Description: A man covered with leprosy approaches Jesus, kneels, and says "If you are willing, you can make me clean." Jesus reaches out his hand, touches him, and says "I am willing. Be clean!" The leprosy leaves him instantly. Jesus instructs him to show himself to the priest as Moses commanded.
    Location: Galilee
    Reference: Matthew 8:1-4; Mark 1:40-45; Luke 5:12-16

15. Healing of a Paralytic (Lowered Through the Roof)
    Description: Four men carry a paralyzed man on a mat to Jesus but cannot get through the crowd. They make an opening in the roof and lower the man down. Jesus first forgives the man's sins (drawing criticism from scribes), then commands him to take up his mat and walk. The man does so, and the crowd glorifies God.
    Location: Capernaum
    Reference: Matthew 9:1-8; Mark 2:1-12; Luke 5:17-26

16. Healing of the Man with a Withered Hand
    Description: In a synagogue on the Sabbath, a man with a withered hand is present. The Pharisees watch to see if Jesus will heal on the Sabbath. Jesus says "Stretch out your hand," and when the man does, his hand is restored whole. This provokes the Pharisees to plot against him.
    Location: Galilee (a synagogue)
    Reference: Matthew 12:9-14; Mark 3:1-6; Luke 6:6-11

17. Healing of the Centurion's Servant
    Description: A Roman centurion in Capernaum sends Jewish elders to ask Jesus to heal his paralyzed servant. Before Jesus arrives, the centurion says he is unworthy to have Jesus under his roof — just say the word and his servant will be healed. Jesus marvels at his faith, saying he has not found such faith in Israel. The servant is healed at that hour.
    Location: Capernaum
    Reference: Matthew 8:5-13; Luke 7:1-10

18. Raising of the Widow's Son at Nain
    Description: Jesus encounters a funeral procession at the town gate of Nain — a widow's only son is being carried out to burial. Jesus has compassion, touches the bier, and says "Young man, I say to you, arise!" The dead man sits up and begins to speak. Fear seizes the crowd, and they say "A great prophet has arisen among us."
    Location: Nain
    Reference: Luke 7:11-17

19. Healing of a Woman with a Hemorrhage
    Description: A woman who has suffered from a bleeding disorder for twelve years touches the fringe of Jesus' garment, thinking "If I only touch his cloak, I will be made well." Immediately her bleeding stops. Jesus feels power leave him and asks who touched him. The woman comes forward in fear, and Jesus says "Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace."
    Location: Galilee (on the way to Jairus' house)
    Reference: Matthew 9:20-22; Mark 5:25-34; Luke 8:43-48

20. Raising of Jairus' Daughter
    Description: Jairus, a synagogue leader, begs Jesus to heal his dying twelve-year-old daughter. While Jesus is delayed by the woman with the hemorrhage, messengers arrive saying the girl has died. Jesus tells Jairus not to fear, only believe. He enters the house, takes the girl's hand, and says "Talitha koum" (Little girl, arise!). Her spirit returns and she gets up and walks.
    Location: Capernaum
    Reference: Matthew 9:18-26; Mark 5:21-43; Luke 8:40-56

21. Healing of Two Blind Men
    Description: After raising Jairus' daughter, two blind men follow Jesus crying out "Have mercy on us, Son of David!" They enter a house, and Jesus asks "Do you believe I am able to do this?" They say yes. He touches their eyes and says "According to your faith let it be done to you," and their sight is restored.
    Location: Capernaum (possibly)
    Reference: Matthew 9:27-31

22. Healing of a Mute Demoniac
    Description: A man who is demon-possessed and unable to speak is brought to Jesus. Jesus casts out the demon, and the man immediately speaks. The crowds marvel, but the Pharisees say "He casts out demons by the prince of demons."
    Location: Galilee
    Reference: Matthew 9:32-34

23. Healing at the Pool of Bethesda
    Description: At the Pool of Bethesda, Jesus encounters a man who has been an invalid for 38 years, lying among many sick people waiting for the stirring of the water. Jesus asks "Do you want to be made well?" and then tells him "Get up, take up your bed, and walk." The man is instantly healed. Because this happens on the Sabbath, the Jews begin to persecute Jesus.
    Location: Jerusalem (Pool of Bethesda)
    Reference: John 5:1-15

24. Healing of the Gerasene Demoniac
    Description: Jesus and his disciples arrive in the region of the Gerasenes (or Gadarenes). A violent demon-possessed man who lives among the tombs runs to Jesus. The legion of demons within him begs Jesus not to torment them and asks to be sent into a herd of pigs. Jesus permits it; the pigs rush down a steep bank into the sea and drown. The man is found clothed and in his right mind.
    Location: Gerasenes/Gadarenes (eastern shore of Sea of Galilee)
    Reference: Matthew 8:28-34; Mark 5:1-20; Luke 8:26-39

25. Healing of a Woman Crippled for 18 Years
    Description: In a synagogue on the Sabbath, Jesus sees a woman bent over and unable to straighten up for 18 years. He calls her over, lays his hands on her, and says "Woman, you are freed from your disability." She immediately straightens up and praises God. The synagogue leader is indignant that Jesus healed on the Sabbath.
    Location: Galilee (a synagogue)
    Reference: Luke 13:10-17

26. Healing of a Man with Dropsy (Edema)
    Description: On the Sabbath, while dining at the house of a prominent Pharisee, a man with dropsy (severe swelling from fluid retention) appears before Jesus. Jesus asks the Pharisees "Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath or not?" When they remain silent, he takes the man, heals him, and sends him away.
    Location: Galilee (a Pharisee's house)
    Reference: Luke 14:1-6

27. Healing of the Man Born Blind
    Description: Jesus encounters a man blind from birth. He makes mud with saliva and dirt, spreads it on the man's eyes, and tells him to wash in the Pool of Siloam. The man does so and receives his sight. This leads to a lengthy interrogation by the Pharisees, who excommunicate the healed man. Jesus later finds him and reveals himself as the Son of Man.
    Location: Jerusalem (Pool of Siloam)
    Reference: John 9:1-41

28. Healing of the Syrophoenician Woman's Daughter
    Description: A Gentile woman from the region of Tyre and Sidon begs Jesus to cast a demon out of her daughter. Jesus initially says "It is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs." The woman replies that even dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their master's table. Jesus commends her great faith and heals her daughter from a distance.
    Location: District of Tyre and Sidon (Phoenicia)
    Reference: Matthew 15:21-28; Mark 7:24-30

29. Healing of a Deaf and Mute Man
    Description: In the Decapolis region, a man who is deaf and barely able to speak is brought to Jesus. Jesus puts his fingers in the man's ears, touches his tongue with saliva, looks up to heaven, sighs, and says "Ephphatha" (Be opened!). The man's ears open, his tongue is loosed, and he speaks plainly.
    Location: Decapolis region
    Reference: Mark 7:31-37

30. Healing of a Blind Man at Bethsaida
    Description: A blind man is brought to Jesus at Bethsaida. Jesus spits on the man's eyes and lays his hands on him. The man sees people "like trees walking." Jesus lays his hands on him again, and his sight is fully restored.
    Location: Bethsaida
    Reference: Mark 8:22-26

31. Healing of a Boy with an Unclean Spirit
    Description: A man brings his son, possessed by a spirit that causes convulsions and foaming at the mouth, to Jesus' disciples but they cannot cast it out. Jesus says "Bring him to me." He rebukes the unclean spirit and commands it never to enter him again. The boy is thrown into a convulsion and then lies still, appearing dead, but Jesus lifts him up. The disciples ask why they could not cast it out; Jesus says "This kind can come out only by prayer (and fasting)."
    Location: Near Mount Tabor / Caesarea Philippi region
    Reference: Matthew 17:14-20; Mark 9:14-29; Luke 9:37-43

32. Healing of Ten Lepers
    Description: On the border between Samaria and Galilee, ten lepers cry out to Jesus for mercy from a distance. He tells them to go show themselves to the priests. As they go, they are cleansed. Only one, a Samaritan, returns to thank Jesus and praise God. Jesus says "Rise and go; your faith has made you well."
    Location: Border of Samaria and Galilee
    Reference: Luke 17:11-19

33. Healing of Bartimaeus (Blind Beggar at Jericho)
    Description: As Jesus leaves Jericho with a large crowd, a blind beggar named Bartimaeus (son of Timaeus) cries out "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" Many tell him to be quiet, but he cries out even louder. Jesus stops and calls him. He asks "What do you want me to do for you?" Bartimaeus says "Rabbi, I want to see again." Jesus says "Go; your faith has made you well," and immediately he receives his sight and follows Jesus.
    Location: Jericho
    Reference: Mark 10:46-52 (also Matthew 20:29-34; Luke 18:35-43)

34. Raising of Lazarus
    Description: Jesus is informed that his beloved friend Lazarus of Bethany is sick. He delays two days, then tells his disciples "Lazarus has died, and for your sake I am glad I was not there, so that you may believe." Upon arriving in Bethany, Lazarus has been in the tomb for four days. Jesus weeps. He then commands the stone to be removed, prays, and cries out "Lazarus, come out!" The dead man emerges, still wrapped in burial cloths. This miracle precipitates the plot to kill Jesus.
    Location: Bethany (near Jerusalem)
    Reference: John 11:1-44

35. Healing of the Deaf Demoniac / Blind and Mute Demoniac
    Description: A demon-possessed man who is both blind and mute is brought to Jesus. He heals him so that he can both speak and see. The crowd is amazed, but the Pharisees again accuse Jesus of using Beelzebul's power.
    Location: Galilee
    Reference: Matthew 12:22-23; Luke 11:14-15

36. Healing of Malchus' Ear (the Servant of the High Priest)
    Description: At the arrest of Jesus, Simon Peter draws his sword and cuts off the right ear of Malchus, the high priest's servant. Jesus tells Peter to put away his sword, touches the man's ear, and heals it — the only miracle recorded during the Passion narrative.
    Location: Garden of Gethsemane (Mount of Olives)
    Reference: Luke 22:50-51; John 18:10-11


NOTES

- The total count of individual miracles of Jesus ranges from 35 to 40 depending on whether parallel accounts of the same event are counted separately. The above list follows scholarly consensus that treats parallel accounts of the same event as a single miracle.
- The Gospel of John refers to Jesus' miracles as "signs" (semeia) and specifically selects seven signs for emphasis, culminating in the raising of Lazarus.
- Jesus performed additional healings described in summary passages (Matthew 4:23-24, 9:35, 14:14, 15:30-31, 19:2, 21:14; Mark 1:32-34, 3:10, 6:5, 6:53-56; Luke 4:40, 5:15, 6:17-19, 7:21, 9:11) where crowds brought many sick people and he healed them all, but these are general summaries rather than specific named incidents.
- The resurrection of Jesus himself is the foundational miracle of Christianity but is distinct from the miracles he performed during his earthly ministry.
- Primary sources: The Gospels according to Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John (RSV-CE, NRSV-CE, and Douay-Rheims translations widely used for cross-reference).
