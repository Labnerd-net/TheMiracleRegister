# Miraculous and Saintly Phenomena — Overview

Sources: Miracle Hunter, Catholic Encyclopedia, various saint biographies
Date compiled: June 6, 2026

---

## Overview

This category covers extraordinary phenomena associated with saints, mystics, and holy places that don't fall under apparitions, Eucharistic miracles, stigmata, incorruptibility, or nature miracles. The Church documents these phenomena but generally doesn't have a formal "approval" process for them as standalone categories. Instead, they are studied as part of canonization causes.

---

## 1. Bilocation (being in two places at once)
Approximately **20-30 reported cases** in Church history.

**Famous examples:**
- **St. Padre Pio** (1887-1968) — Dozens of documented cases of being seen in two places simultaneously (heard confessions in Italy while appearing to an ill man in South America, etc.)
- **St. Martin de Porres** (1579-1639, Lima, Peru) — Bilocated to China, Japan, Africa, and other countries to help the poor
- **St. Francis Xavier** (1506-1552) — Bilocated to preach while physically at sea
- **St. Anthony of Padua** (1195-1231) — Preached in two places at once
- **St. Gerard Majella** (1726-1755) — Bilocated while sick in bed
- **Ven. Maria of Agreda** (1602-1665) — Reports of bilocating to New Mexico to preach to Native Americans while physically in Spain
- **St. Alphonsus Liguori** (1696-1787) — Bilocated from his monastery to the deathbed of Pope Clement XIV

---

## 2. Levitation
Approximately **20+ documented saints** known to have levitated.

**Famous examples:**
- **St. Joseph of Cupertino** (1603-1663, Italy) — The most famous — levitated dozens of times during Mass and prayer; sometimes rose so high the others had to pull him down
- **St. Teresa of Avila** (1515-1582, Spain) — Documented levitations during ecstatic prayer
- **St. John of the Cross** (1542-1591) — Levitated during Mass
- **St. Francis of Assisi** (1181-1226) — Levitated while praying
- **St. Philip Neri** (1515-1595) — Levitated (often into tree branches)
- **St. Alphonsus Liguori** (1696-1787) — Levitated while preaching
- **St. Gemma Galgani** (1878-1903)
- **Bl. Anne Catherine Emmerich** (1774-1824)

---

## 3. Inedia (living without food, solely on the Eucharist)
Approximately **10-15 documented cases** over Church history.

**Examples:**
- **St. Catherine of Siena** (1347-1380) — Lived almost exclusively on the Eucharist for years
- **St. Nicholas of Flue** (1417-1487, Switzerland) — Ate no food for 19 years except the Eucharist
- **St. Lidwina of Schiedam** (1380-1433) — Lived on the Eucharist for decades
- **St. Rose of Lima** (1586-1617) — Minimal food, only the Eucharist
- **Bl. Anne Catherine Emmerich** (1774-1824) — Lived on only Eucharist and water for years
- **Therese Neumann** (1898-1962, Konnersreuth, Germany) — Lived on only the Eucharist for 36 years, documented by physicians
- **Marthe Robin** (1902-1981, France) — Lived on only the Eucharist for 50+ years, medically documented
- **Bl. Alexandrina da Costa** (1904-1955, Portugal) — Lived on only the Eucharist for 13+ years, documented
- **Luisa Piccarreta** (1865-1947, Italy) — Lived on only the Eucharist for decades

## 4. Luminosity / Supernatural Light
Saints seen surrounded by light, or emitting light from their bodies. Occasionally called "photisms."

**Examples:**
- **St. Francis of Assisi** — witnesses saw him surrounded by a bright cloud while praying
- **St. Padre Pio** — often seen praying in a bright light
- **St. Teresa of Avila** — her face radiated light during ecstasy
- **St. Philip Neri** — his room was seen filled with heavenly light
- **St. Martin de Porres** — luminous phenomena in his cell
- **St. John Bosco** — his face shone during Mass

---

## 5. Weeping / Bleeding Statues and Images
One of the most common modern miraculous phenomena. The Church investigates each case thoroughly.

**Notable cases with Church recognition:**
- **Our Lady of Akita** (1973, Japan) — A wooden statue wept 101 times; also shed blood; approved by local bishop
- **Our Lady of Syracuse** (1953, Italy) — A statue of the Immaculate Heart wept; examined by scientists, declared miraculous
- **Our Lady of Civitavecchia** (1995, Italy) — Statue wept blood; DNA testing inconclusive (human blood, no match to anyone known)
- **Our Lady of Tre Fontane** (Rome, 1947) — Approved for faith expression
- **Our Lady of Naju** (Korea) — Weeping statue, but not officially approved

**Note:** The Church receives thousands of claims of weeping statues. Only a handful have been formally approved or deemed worthy of belief.

## 6. Prophecy / Private Revelations
Messages received by visionaries, approved or not. See the Marian Apparitions note for the formal approval framework.

**Notable approved private revelations beyond apparitions:**
- The Sacred Heart revelations to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque (1673-1675)
- The Divine Mercy revelations to St. Faustina Kowalska (1930s)
- The messages of the Divine Will to Luisa Piccarreta (in progress, not fully approved)

---

## 7. Odor of Sanctity
A supernatural fragrance often reported at the death of saints or emanating from their relics.

**Examples:**
- **St. Teresa of Avila** — her body emitted a fragrance described as lily-like
- **St. John Bosco** — his relics were known for a sweet fragrance
- **St. Padre Pio** — his stigmata wounds had a fragrance described as violet-like (in contrast to the foul odor of natural wounds)
- **St. Charbel Makhlouf** — his body exuded a blood-like liquid with a distinct scent
- **St. Gerard Majella** — body emitted a sweet fragrance

## Notes
- These phenomena are not "approved" as a category in the way apparitions or Eucharistic miracles are
- They are documented as part of canonization processes or local devotional practices
- The Church is cautious: many claims of miraculous phenomena do not pass scrutiny
- Bilocation, levitation, and inedia are extremely rare; the Church treats them with great caution even when historically well-documented
