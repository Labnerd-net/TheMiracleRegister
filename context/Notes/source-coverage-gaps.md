# Source Coverage Gaps

Living tracker for records whose sourcing doesn't yet meet the standard defined in the `verify-sources` skill's coverage mode (`.claude/skills/verify-sources/SKILL.md`). Unlike `dead-links-archive.md`, gaps here don't decay with time — a record stays open until someone adds the missing source in the database. Consolidates and supersedes the now-deleted `Missing Primary Sources.md` and `Source Requirements Audit - 2026-09-29.md`; their content is folded in below.

**When re-running coverage mode:** anything below no longer reproducing moves to `## Resolved` with today's date. Anything new gets added under the relevant heading with today's date as "first flagged." Don't re-open a `## Resolved` entry unless it's genuinely regressed (note why, if apparent).

**2026-09-29 enrichment (doesn't close the gap, but adds a real corroborating source):** vatican.va's press office (`press.vatican.va`, a genuine `.vatican.va` subdomain) publishes "Promulgazione di Decreti della Congregazione/Dicastero delle Cause dei Santi" bulletins each time a miracle decree is authorized. ### Saints missing the status leg (a vatican.va document) — 1

First flagged 2026-09-29 (11). 9 resolved 2026-09-29 by adding each saint's canonization/beatification homily hosted on vatican.va (`## Resolved`). A 10th, `bernadette-soubirous`, initially looked like a genuine pre-digital-era gap (no 1933 Pius XI homily is hosted on vatican.va) but was resolved the same day via a different route: **vatican.va hosts a full digitized Acta Apostolicae Sedis (AAS) archive** at `vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/` going back to the 1920s (large per-year OCR-scanned PDFs). AAS Vol. XXVI (1933-1934) contains the actual canonization Bull (*Litterae Decretales*) naming her directly — confirmed by downloading the PDF and running it through `pdftotext` locally (WebFetch alone couldn't extract readable text from the raw scan). Added as `vatican_decree`. **Worth remembering for future gaps**: AAS on vatican.va is a real avenue for saints canonized before homilies were routinely published/hosted — check it before concluding a pre-1960s canonization has no vatican.va document at all.

1 remains a genuine gap:

- `fulton-sheen` — beatified 24 September 2026. Modern beatifications aren't presided over by the Pope (a cardinal delegate reads the decree), so there's no papal homily to host, and AAS is published with a lag of months to years — a September 2026 beatification's decree isn't in a digitized AAS volume yet. The decree itself is announced via `causesanti.va` and covered by `vaticannews.va`/Catholic press, but neither is `vatican.va` proper. Flagging as a borderline case per `SKILL.md`'s instruction to flag rather than silently decide — `causesanti.va` is a genuine Holy See domain but not `vatican.va`, so it doesn't literally clear the bar as currently written. Revisit once the relevant AAS volume is digitized (likely 1-2+ years out).

**On whether AAS also unlocks the 41-item Tier 1 bucket (miracle recipients not named in homilies):** checked this directly — it doesn't, structurally, not just as a matter of searching harder. The Congregation for the Causes of Saints' *"Decretum super miraculo"* entries (the formal miracle-approval decrees, which do sometimes appear in AAS or its `press.vatican.va` consistory announcements) follow a fixed formula naming only the **Blessed/Venerable and the date** — e.g. "a miracle attributed to the intercession of Blessed Augustine Roscelli" — never the healed person. That's true even in older/pre-privacy-era examples pulled during this check. So unlike Bernadette's case (a saint's *own* canonization Bull, which by definition names her), there's no equivalent AAS document that names an individual miracle *recipient* — the Escrivá-style dedicated page remains the rare, deliberate exception, not something findable via a different archive.
